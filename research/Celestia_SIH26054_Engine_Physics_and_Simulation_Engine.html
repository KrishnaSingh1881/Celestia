Everything the Celestia twin needs to compute: the engine's governing equations, how each failure mode perturbs them, how we generate fault data without a real engine, and how residuals become a Remaining Useful Life number a commander can act on.

This report is the engineering layer under the Celestia solution brief. It is written so that a reader can stop at any bold sentence and still carry away the point, and so that an engineer implementing the simulator can lift the equations directly into code.

Three questions organise everything that follows. What is physically happening inside a MALE-UAV piston engine? What changes physically when a component starts to fail? How do we reproduce both in software accurately enough that a model trained on it works on a real engine? Part II answers the first, Part III the second, Parts IV–V the third.

The one idea the whole system rests on A failing component does not announce itself. It changes a physical coefficient — a sealing area, a heat-transfer coefficient, a friction term, a flow resistance. That coefficient change propagates through the governing equations and shifts what the sensors read. If we own the equations, we can invert the chain: measure the shift, infer the coefficient, name the component.

§1Platform & engine context

1 · What the engine is asked to do

A MALE UAV engine is not a performance engine. It is a low-power, extremely long-duty-cycle machine that must hold one throttle setting for hours in thin, cold air, with nobody on board to listen for a change in note.

1.1 The duty cycle drives the monitoring problem

A typical sortie is climb to 15–25 000 ft, then 8–24 h at a near-constant cruise power, then descent. Three consequences follow, and all three shape the twin:

  • Degradation is slow and monotonic, not dramatic. Over an 8 h flight a real fault moves a temperature by a few degrees per hour. It is invisible against a fixed red-line and obvious against a prediction.
  • Operating point is almost constant, so the model does not need to be accurate across the whole map — it needs to be extremely accurate near cruise, which is a far easier target.
  • There is no pilot in the loop. Every inference must survive on telemetry and must degrade gracefully when the link does.

1.2 Reference engine

We anchor the physics on the Rotax 914-class engine — the turbocharged four-cylinder boxer used on MALE-class platforms including the MQ-1 Predator lineage. Published data give us the hard constraints a physics model must reproduce.

§2Architecture & sensing

2 · Architecture and the sensing layer

2.1 Where the engine sits in the aircraft

The propulsion system is one branch of the platform, but it is the branch that ends the mission when it fails. The twin therefore needs both engine state and the flight context that explains it: without altitude, airspeed and throttle demand, a rise in cylinder head temperature is uninterpretable.

2.2 Engine subsystems and their coupling

Six subsystems exchange energy and mass continuously. Most failure cascades in Part III are simply energy or mass leaking across one of these interfaces.

2.3 The control loop we are shadowing

A FADEC or TCU already closes a fast loop around the engine. Celestia does not join that loop — it observes it. This is a safety decision as much as an engineering one: the twin is an advisory system that never has authority over an actuator.

At 16-bit that is ≈ 6 kB/s per axis before overhead — an uplink cost no UAV RF budget will pay for hours at a time. The resolution is to move the transform to the engine. 2.5 Three ways a sensor failure hurts

Carried over from our sensor study and used directly in §22:

  • Type 1 — control information lost. The controller can no longer close its loop correctly (crank position, fuel pressure). Highest severity, but usually self-evident: the engine misbehaves.
  • Type 2 — a dangerous condition is hidden. The sensor reads plausible while the real quantity is out of limits (oil pressure sensor stuck at "normal" during an actual pressure loss). This is the dangerous class, and it is exactly what analytical redundancy is for.
  • Type 3 — monitoring capability lost. The engine runs fine; we simply become blind to one fault family (vibration channel dead). Degrades confidence, not safety.
§3Kinematics & the cycle

3 · Kinematics and the thermodynamic cycle

Everything downstream — power, temperature, vibration, wear — is a consequence of one geometric function and one energy balance. Get these two right and the rest is bookkeeping.

3.1 The volume function

Crank radius \(a=S/2\), connecting-rod length \(l\), bore \(B\), crank angle \(\theta\) measured from top dead centre. The piston's distance from the crank axis is the projection of the crank plus the rod:

\[ s(\theta)=a\cos\theta+\sqrt{l^{2}-a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta} \]
(3.1)

so the instantaneous cylinder volume, with clearance volume \(V_c=V_d/(r_c-1)\), is

\[ V(\theta)=V_c+\frac{\pi B^{2}}{4}\Big[(l+a)-s(\theta)\Big] \]
(3.2)

and its derivative, needed at every integration step for the work term:

\[ \frac{dV}{d\theta}=\frac{\pi B^{2}}{4}\,a\sin\theta\left[1+\frac{a\cos\theta}{\sqrt{l^{2}-a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}}\right] \]
(3.3)

Expanding (3.1) for \(\lambda_r = a/l \ll 1\) gives the form used in the vibration model, where the second-order term is the source of the characteristic 2nd-order shake of a reciprocating engine:

\[ \ddot{x}\simeq a\omega^{2}\left(\cos\theta+\lambda_r\cos 2\theta\right) \]
(3.4)

Heat-transfer area is head + crown + exposed liner, and it breathes with the piston:

\[ A(\theta)=\frac{\pi B^{2}}{2}+\pi B\big[(l+a)-s(\theta)\big] \]
(3.5)

3.2 The ideal bound, and why we do not stop there

For an ideal Otto cycle with constant \(\gamma\), heat is added at constant volume between states 2 and 3 and rejected at constant volume between 4 and 1. Thermal efficiency is

\[ \eta_{\text{Otto}}=1-\frac{T_4-T_1}{T_3-T_2}=1-r_c^{\,1-\gamma} \]
(3.6)

using \(T_2/T_1=T_3/T_4=r_c^{\gamma-1}\) from the isentropic relations. At \(r_c = 9\) and \(\gamma=1.35\) this gives ≈ 55 %, while the real engine delivers about 30 %. That 25-point gap is not error — it is the physics that carries the diagnostic information: finite burn duration, wall heat loss, friction, pumping, blow-by. A twin built on (3.6) would be blind to every fault we care about.

3.3 The governing ODE

Apply the first law to the cylinder contents treated as a single zone of ideal gas, with mass \(m\), internal energy \(u\), and heat crossing the boundary:

\[ \frac{d(mu)}{d\theta}= \frac{dQ_{\text{ch}}}{d\theta}-\frac{dQ_{\text{ht}}}{d\theta}-p\frac{dV}{d\theta}+h_{\text{bb}}\frac{dm}{d\theta} \]
(3.7)

Substituting \(u=c_vT\), \(pV=mRT\) and rearranging gives the two states we actually integrate:

\[ \frac{dT}{d\theta}=\frac{1}{m c_v}\left[\frac{dQ_{\text{ch}}}{d\theta}-\frac{dQ_{\text{ht}}}{d\theta}-p\frac{dV}{d\theta}\right]-\frac{T}{m}\frac{dm}{d\theta} \]
(3.8)
\[ \frac{dp}{d\theta}=p\left[\frac{1}{m}\frac{dm}{d\theta}+\frac{1}{T}\frac{dT}{d\theta}-\frac{1}{V}\frac{dV}{d\theta}\right] \]
(3.9)

Equivalently, in the compact form often quoted:

\[ \frac{dp}{d\theta}=-\gamma\frac{p}{V}\frac{dV}{d\theta}+\frac{\gamma-1}{V}\left(\frac{dQ_{\text{ch}}}{d\theta}-\frac{dQ_{\text{ht}}}{d\theta}\right) \]
(3.10)

Three source terms sit on the right-hand side, and each one is a different family of fault: \(dQ_{\text{ch}}\) carries combustion and fuel faults (§4), \(dQ_{\text{ht}}\) carries cooling and deposit faults (§5), and \(dm/d\theta\) carries sealing faults — rings, valves, gaskets (§6.3). We integrate from intake-valve closing to exhaust-valve opening, the closed period, where the mass boundary is simple.

Specific heats are not constant across 300–2800 K, so we carry a linear \(\gamma(T)\) and derive \(c_v = R/(\gamma-1)\) at each step. This is the cheapest correction with the largest accuracy return.

§4Combustion

4 · Combustion

4.1 Heat release and the Wiebe law

We do not resolve flame propagation. We prescribe how fast chemical energy appears, using the Wiebe function — an empirical S-curve whose three parameters are physically interpretable and, crucially, fault-sensitive:

\[ x_b(\theta)=1-\exp\left[-a_w\left(\frac{\theta-\theta_0}{\Delta\theta}\right)^{m_w+1}\right] \]
(4.1)
\[ \frac{dx_b}{d\theta}=\frac{a_w(m_w+1)}{\Delta\theta}\left(\frac{\theta-\theta_0}{\Delta\theta}\right)^{m_w}\big[1-x_b(\theta)\big] \]
(4.2)

with \(\theta_0\) the start of combustion, \(\Delta\theta\) the burn duration, \(a_w \approx 5\) (99 % burn completeness) and \(m_w \approx 2\) the shape factor. Chemical heat release is then

\[ \frac{dQ_{\text{ch}}}{d\theta}=m_f\,\text{LHV}\,\eta_c\,\frac{dx_b}{d\theta} \]
(4.3)

4.2 Mixture

Trapped fuel mass follows from the trapped charge and the equivalence ratio. With stoichiometric air–fuel ratio \(\text{AFR}_s \approx 14.6\) for avgas/mogas, relative air–fuel ratio \(\lambda = \text{AFR}/\text{AFR}_s\) and \(\phi = 1/\lambda\):

\[ m_f=\frac{m_{\text{tot}}}{1+\lambda\,\text{AFR}_s},\qquad m_{f,\text{burn}}=\min\!\left(m_f,\ \frac{m_{\text{air}}}{\text{AFR}_s}\right) \]
(4.4)

The min matters. Aero engines run deliberately rich at high power (\(\lambda\approx0.85\)) to suppress detonation and cool the charge; the excess fuel cannot burn for want of oxygen, so it must not be counted as heat. Omitting this term was the single largest error in our first calibration pass — it over-predicted take-off power by 19 %.

4.3 Knock margin

Detonation is an autoignition race: the end-gas must not reach its ignition delay before the flame arrives. The Livengood–Wu integral accumulates that risk over the compression and burn:

\[ I_{k}=\int_{\theta_{\text{IVC}}}^{\theta_{\text{eoc}}}\frac{d\theta}{\omega\,\tau(p,T)},\qquad \tau = A\,p^{-n}\exp\!\left(\frac{B_{a}}{T}\right) \]
(4.5)

Knock is predicted when \(I_k \to 1\). This is what makes charge temperature a safety variable, not a comfort variable: the airbox temperature limit on a turbocharged Rotax exists because \(\tau\) collapses exponentially with \(T\). A degraded intercooler and a stuck wastegate reach the same place by different roads, and the twin can tell them apart because one moves \(T\) at constant boost and the other moves both.

4.4 Misfire, in model terms

A misfire is not a special case needing special code. It is \(x_b \equiv 0\) for one cylinder on one cycle. The consequences follow automatically from §3: no pressure rise, negative indicated work on that cycle, unburned charge dumped into the exhaust where it may burn late and spike EGT, and a torque hole that appears in the crank-speed signal once per two revolutions — the half-order signature of §9.

§5Heat transfer & thermal network

5 · Heat transfer and the thermal network

CHT, EGT, oil and coolant temperature are the richest fault channels on the engine. They are all downstream of one term: where the combustion heat goes.

5.1 In-cylinder heat transfer — Woschni

Convective loss to the walls is modelled with the Woschni correlation, in which the effective gas velocity has a piston-driven term and a combustion-driven term:

\[ h_g=3.26\,B^{-0.2}\,p^{0.8}\,T^{-0.55}\,w^{0.8}\quad[\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}K^{-1}},\ p\ \text{in kPa}] \]
(5.1)
\[ w=C_1\bar{S}_p+C_2\frac{V_dT_1}{p_1V_1}\big(p-p_{\text{mot}}\big) \]
(5.2)

\(C_1 = 2.28\) over the closed period, \(C_2 = 3.24\times10^{-3}\) once combustion starts, \(\bar{S}_p = 2SN\) is mean piston speed, and \(p_{\text{mot}}\) is the motored (no-combustion) pressure at the same crank angle. The heat flow entering the ODE is

\[ \frac{dQ_{\text{ht}}}{d\theta}=\frac{h_g\,A(\theta)\,\big(T-T_w\big)}{\omega} \]
(5.3)

5.2 From gas to metal: the lumped thermal network

The cylinder head has thermal mass, so it integrates the heat flux rather than following it. That integration is why CHT is such a good slow-fault detector — it is a natural low-pass filter on combustion anomalies. Each node obeys a lumped-capacitance balance:

\[ m_ic_{p,i}\frac{dT_i}{dt}=\dot{Q}_{\text{in},i}-\sum_j \frac{T_i-T_j}{R_{ij}}-h_iA_i\big(T_i-T_\infty\big) \]
(5.4)

with node time constant

\[ \tau_i=\frac{m_ic_{p,i}}{\sum_j R_{ij}^{-1}+h_iA_i} \]
(5.5)

Four nodes are enough: head metal (\(\tau\approx\)30–90 s), coolant (\(\tau\approx\)60–150 s), oil (\(\tau\approx\)5–15 min), cylinder wall/liner. The external coefficient \(h_i\) is where the environment enters: for the air-cooled cylinders it scales with cooling-air mass flow, which scales with airspeed and density, so altitude and airspeed must be inputs to the thermal model or every climb looks like a cooling fault.

Different time constants are free information Because the nodes have separated time constants, the order in which temperatures move identifies the cause. Combustion anomaly: EGT leads, CHT follows, oil last. Cooling-circuit degradation: coolant and CHT rise together with EGT unchanged. Lubrication fault: oil temperature rises with oil pressure falling while EGT and coolant stay flat. The lag structure is a fingerprint, and it is only visible to a model that has the time constants in it.

5.3 The EGT observation model

A common modelling mistake is to compare the in-cylinder temperature at exhaust-valve opening with the EGT the sensor reports. They differ by hundreds of kelvin. Our model solves \(T_{\text{EVO}}\), then applies two physical steps. Blowdown expansion to exhaust pressure:

\[ T_{\text{bd}}=T_{\text{EVO}}\left(\frac{p_{\text{exh}}}{p_{\text{EVO}}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}} \]
(5.6)

then convective loss along the port and pipe to the probe location, as a heat exchanger of finite effectiveness:

\[ T_{\text{probe}}=T_{\text{pipe}}+\big(T_{\text{bd}}-T_{\text{pipe}}\big)\exp\!\left(-\frac{h_pA_p}{\dot{m}c_p}\right) \]
(5.7)

With our baseline case \(T_{\text{EVO}} = 2148\) K and \(p_{\text{EVO}} = 8.6\) bar, (5.6) gives ≈ 1520 K and (5.7) lands the probe near 1150 K (≈ 880 °C) — the right neighbourhood for a Rotax EGT limit. The exponential term also explains a real operational effect: EGT indication depends on mass flow, so the same combustion state reads differently at different power settings. A twin that models the probe, not just the gas, does not mistake that for a fault.

§6Gas exchange & turbocharging

6 · Gas exchange, sealing and turbocharging

6.1 How much air actually gets in

Volumetric efficiency \(\eta_v\) is the ratio of the charge actually trapped to the displacement filled at manifold conditions. For a four-stroke engine the air mass flow is

\[ \dot{m}_a=\eta_v\,\rho_i\,V_d\,\frac{N}{2},\qquad \rho_i=\frac{p_{\text{MAP}}}{R\,T_i} \]
(6.1)

This is the speed-density relation, and it is the backbone of the real-time mean-value model in §13. \(\eta_v\) is itself a function of speed and pressure ratio, stored as a calibrated surface \(\eta_v(N, p_{\text{MAP}}/p_{\text{exh}})\). A drop in \(\eta_v\) at a known speed and pressure is a direct measurement of breathing degradation — fouled filter, valve-seat recession, wrong valve clearance.

6.2 Flow through a restriction

Valves, wastegates, injectors, leak paths and blow-by are all the same object to the model: a compressible flow through an effective area. Two regimes, one formula:

\[ \dot{m}=C_dA\,\frac{p_u}{\sqrt{RT_u}}\,\Psi\!\left(\frac{p_d}{p_u}\right) \]
(6.2)
\[ \Psi(\pi)=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\gamma\left(\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1}}}, & \pi\le\pi_{\text{crit}}\\\sqrt{\dfrac{2\gamma}{\gamma-1}\left(\pi^{\frac{2}{\gamma}}-\pi^{\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma}}\right)}, & \pi>\pi_{\text{crit}}\end{cases} \]
(6.3)

with \(\pi_{\text{crit}}=\left(\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\right)^{\gamma/(\gamma-1)}\approx 0.528\). One implementation serves every flow path in the engine; a fault is just a different \(C_dA\).

6.3 Blow-by: the sealing fault, expressed physically

Worn rings or a leaking valve open an effective area to the crankcase. Because the pressure ratio across that area is enormous near TDC, the leak is strongly gated by peak pressure:

\[ \frac{dm}{d\theta}\bigg|_{\text{bb}}=-\frac{C_dA_{\text{bb}}}{\omega}\frac{p}{\sqrt{RT}}\Psi\!\left(\frac{p_{\text{cc}}}{p}\right) \]
(6.4)

This single term explains an entire fault family at once: compression loss, peak-pressure droop, reduced indicated work, crankcase pressurisation, oil contamination and — because the leaked charge carries enthalpy out of the cylinder and burns late — a rise in EGT alongside a fall in peak pressure. That combination, falling \(p_{\max}\) with rising EGT, is a signature no single-sensor rule can express.

6.4 Turbocharging

The compressor raises intake pressure; the turbine takes the energy from the exhaust. Compressor outlet temperature with isentropic efficiency \(\eta_c\):

\[ T_{2}=T_{1}\left[1+\frac{1}{\eta_c}\left(\Pi_c^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}-1\right)\right],\qquad \dot{W}_c=\dot{m}_ac_p\big(T_2-T_1\big) \]
(6.5)
\[ \dot{W}_t=\eta_t\,\dot{m}_ec_{p,e}T_3\left[1-\Pi_t^{-\frac{\gamma_e-1}{\gamma_e}}\right] \]
(6.6)

and the shaft ties them together, with the wastegate bleeding \(\dot{m}_{\text{wg}}\) around the turbine to regulate boost:

\[ J_{tc}\,\omega_{tc}\frac{d\omega_{tc}}{dt}=\eta_m\dot{W}_t-\dot{W}_c \]
(6.7)

Turbo faults enter as reductions in \(\eta_c\), \(\eta_t\) or increases in \(J_{tc}\) bearing drag. Each has a different fingerprint in the residual set: fouling moves \(\eta_c\) and therefore boost at given turbo speed; bearing wear moves the transient spool-up rate; seal failure shows as oil consumption plus boost deficit.

6.5 Altitude — why every baseline must be corrected

The standard atmosphere in the troposphere gives

\[ T_{\text{amb}}=T_0-Lh,\qquad p_{\text{amb}}=p_0\left(\frac{T_{\text{amb}}}{T_0}\right)^{\frac{g}{RL}},\qquad \rho=\frac{p_{\text{amb}}}{RT_{\text{amb}}} \]
(6.8)

with \(L = 6.5\) K/km and \(g/RL \approx 5.256\). A naturally aspirated engine loses power roughly with density; the turbo holds manifold pressure constant by closing the wastegate progressively, until at the critical altitude the wastegate is shut and boost falls with ambient thereafter.

The altitude trap in threshold monitoring Between sea level and cruise altitude, ambient pressure halves, ambient temperature falls 40 K, cooling-air density drops, and the turbo works progressively harder for the same boost. Every engine parameter legitimately moves. A fixed threshold must therefore be set wide enough to tolerate all of it — which is exactly how much fault it will also tolerate. Context-conditioned baselines recover the sensitivity that fixed limits throw away (§19).
§7Friction, torque, shaft

7 · From cylinder pressure to propeller thrust

7.1 Mean effective pressures

Indicated work per cycle is the closed-loop integral of pressure over volume; dividing by displacement gives a pressure-like quantity independent of engine size:

\[ W_i=\oint p\,dV,\qquad \text{IMEP}=\frac{W_i}{V_d} \]
(7.1)

Subtract the pumping loop and the mechanical losses to reach what the shaft actually delivers:

\[ \text{BMEP}=\text{IMEP}_{\text{gross}}-\text{PMEP}-\text{FMEP} \]
(7.2)

7.2 Friction — Chen–Flynn

Mechanical friction is modelled with the Chen–Flynn decomposition, which splits losses into a constant term, a peak-pressure-driven term (bearing and ring load), and terms linear and quadratic in piston speed (hydrodynamic shear and windage):

\[ \text{FMEP}=A_f+B_f\,p_{\max}+C_f\,\bar{S}_p+D_f\,\bar{S}_p^{2} \]
(7.3)

This is the term that bearing wear, oil-film loss and gearbox degradation push on. Because \(B_f\) multiplies peak pressure and \(C_f, D_f\) multiply speed, a friction increase has a different speed–load footprint than a combustion loss of the same magnitude in power — which is what lets the diagnosis layer separate them (§23).

7.3 Torque, power and the shaft

\[ T_b=\frac{\text{BMEP}\cdot V_d}{4\pi}\ \ \text{(4-stroke)},\qquad P_b=2\pi N T_b=\text{BMEP}\cdot V_d\cdot\frac{N}{2} \]
(7.4)

The crankshaft is then a rotational inertia driven by the difference between engine torque and the load the propeller imposes through the reduction gearbox of ratio \(i\) and efficiency \(\eta_g\):

\[ J_{\text{eff}}\frac{d\omega}{dt}=T_b(\theta,\omega,\mathbf{u})-\frac{T_{\text{prop}}}{i\,\eta_g},\qquad J_{\text{eff}}=J_e+\frac{J_p}{i^{2}} \]
(7.5)

7.4 Propeller load

Thrust and torque follow the standard non-dimensional forms, with advance ratio \(J=V_\infty/(n D)\):

\[ T=C_T(J,\beta)\,\rho n^{2}D^{4},\qquad Q=C_Q(J,\beta)\,\rho n^{2}D^{5},\qquad \eta_p=\frac{J\,C_T}{2\pi C_Q} \]
(7.6)

The operating point is where engine torque and propeller torque balance — solved each timestep by a scalar Newton iteration on \(\omega\). Two operational facts fall out of this and both matter for diagnosis: air density appears in the load, so the same throttle gives a different speed at altitude; and with a fixed-pitch propeller, any loss of engine torque shows up first as a loss of RPM, not a loss of thrust, which makes crank speed at fixed throttle a sensitive, cheap health channel.

§8Lubrication & tribology

8 · Lubrication and tribology

Lubrication failure is the fastest path from "a parameter moved" to "the engine is destroyed". It is also the failure mode whose physics is most often modelled as a threshold on oil pressure — which is the one thing oil pressure alone cannot tell you.

8.1 Pressure is a balance, not a property

A positive-displacement pump delivers a flow proportional to speed, less internal leakage that rises with pressure and falls with viscosity:

\[ Q_{\text{pump}}=\eta_{\text{vol}}D_pN-\frac{k_l\,\Delta p}{\mu} \]
(8.1)

The gallery network resists that flow. In the laminar regime each path obeys Hagen–Poiseuille, so the circuit behaves as a viscosity-dependent resistance:

\[ Q=\frac{\pi d^{4}\Delta p}{128\,\mu L}\quad\Rightarrow\quad R_h=\frac{128\mu L}{\pi d^{4}},\qquad p_{\text{oil}}=R_{h,\text{eff}}\,Q_{\text{pump}} \]
(8.2)

Read (8.1) and (8.2) together and the diagnostic structure appears. Oil pressure can fall for four physically distinct reasons: pump wear (\(\eta_{\text{vol}}\downarrow\)), bearing clearance growth (\(d\uparrow\), so \(R_h\downarrow\)), a leak (mass loss), or simply hot thin oil (\(\mu\downarrow\)). Only the last is benign, and only a model that carries \(\mu(T)\) can subtract it.

8.2 Viscosity — the Vogel relation

\[ \mu(T)=K\exp\!\left(\frac{\theta_1}{T+\theta_2}\right) \]
(8.3)

Viscosity is exponential in temperature: across a normal operating band it changes by an order of magnitude. This is why oil pressure and oil temperature must be interpreted as a pair, never separately.

8.3 Film thickness and the cliff

Whether a journal bearing survives depends on whether a hydrodynamic film separates the surfaces. The governing group is the Sommerfeld number, with journal radius \(R\), radial clearance \(c\), speed \(N\) and specific load \(P\):

\[ S=\frac{\mu N}{P}\left(\frac{R}{c}\right)^{2} \]
(8.4)

Minimum film thickness scales as \(h_{\min}=c\,(1-\varepsilon)\) with eccentricity \(\varepsilon\) a decreasing function of \(S\). Three of the four terms in (8.4) are degradation-sensitive: \(\mu\) falls as oil ages and heats, \(c\) grows as the bearing wears, \(P\) rises with peak cylinder pressure. They act multiplicatively, which is why bearing failure is characteristically sudden after a long quiet period — the engine walks down the Stribeck curve smoothly and then falls off it.

8.4 The oil as a thermal sensor

Oil temperature is not merely a limit to respect; it is a calorimeter reading friction power:

\[ m_oc_o\frac{dT_o}{dt}=\underbrace{\dot{Q}_{\text{fric}}}_{=\,\text{FMEP}\cdot V_d N/2}+\dot{Q}_{\text{piston}}-\dot{Q}_{\text{cooler}} \]
(8.5)

An increase in bearing friction of a few hundred watts is invisible in shaft power (it is 0.3 % of output) but shows as a persistent few-kelvin offset in oil temperature once \(\tau_{\text{oil}}\) has elapsed. The oil node amplifies what the power channel hides.

§9Vibration

9 · Vibration and order analysis

9.1 Where the torque ripple comes from

Instantaneous crank torque is the sum of a gas term and a reciprocating-inertia term:

\[ T(\theta)=\underbrace{\big[p(\theta)-p_{\text{cc}}\big]\frac{\pi B^{2}}{4}\,r(\theta)}_{\text{gas}}\;-\;\underbrace{m_{\text{rec}}\,\ddot{x}(\theta)\,r(\theta)}_{\text{inertia}} \]
(9.1)

where \(r(\theta)=dV/d\theta \cdot 4/(\pi B^2)\) is the effective crank arm. Substituting the expansion (3.4) shows the inertia term contains \(\cos\theta\) and \(\cos2\theta\) components — the first- and second-order excitations that dominate a four-cylinder engine's casing vibration even when it is perfectly healthy.

9.2 Orders, not frequencies

Everything mechanical in the engine is phase-locked to the crank, so we work in engine orders — multiples of the crank rotation frequency \(f_0=N/60\). Speed changes then do not smear the diagnosis:

The simulator generates vibration as a deterministic order series plus an impulsive component convolved with a structural resonance, plus noise:

\[ v(t)=\sum_k A_k\sin\big(2\pi k f_0 t+\varphi_k\big)+\sum_j \delta(t-t_j)\ast g(t)+\varepsilon(t) \]
(9.3)
\[ g(t)=e^{-\zeta\omega_n t}\sin\big(\omega_n\sqrt{1-\zeta^{2}}\,t\big) \]
(9.4)

Faults are injected by changing \(A_k\) at the relevant orders (misfire adds the half-order family; imbalance grows 1×; gear wear grows mesh sidebands) or by adding an impulse train at a defect frequency. Since the fault is defined in the same space the detector works in, training labels are exact by construction.

Edge feature vector (transmitted at 1–2 Hz)

RMS and peak of each axis; crest factor; kurtosis; band energies at 0.5×, 1×, 1.5×, 2×, 2.5×, 4×, mesh order; half-order energy ratio \(E_{0.5}/E_{2}\); spectral centroid; envelope-spectrum peaks at BPFO/BPFI with their amplitudes; cyclostationary indicator. Twenty-one floats, ≈ 84 bytes per frame — the entire vibration diagnostic, at 0.02 % of the raw data rate.

§10Degradation laws

10 · Degradation: the slow-time dynamics

Sections 3–9 describe milliseconds to minutes. Prognosis lives in hours to hundreds of hours. The link is that fast-time coefficients are slow-time states.

10.1 Time-scale separation

Write the model as a fast system with a slowly varying parameter vector:

\[ \dot{\mathbf{x}}=f\big(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u},\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\big),\qquad \dot{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}=\epsilon\,g\big(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u},\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\big),\qquad \epsilon\ll1 \]
(10.1)

\(\mathbf{x}\) is pressure, temperature, speed. \(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\) is clearance, effective sealing area, heat-transfer effectiveness, injector flow coefficient, compressor efficiency. Diagnosis estimates \(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\); prognosis integrates \(g\). This is the formal statement of what our system does, and it is why fault detection and RUL are the same model, not two.

10.2 Wear — Archard

\[ \frac{dV_w}{ds}=k\frac{W}{H}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{dc}{dt}=\frac{k}{H}\,\frac{W\,\bar{S}_p}{A_{\text{app}}}\,\Phi(\text{lubrication regime}) \]
(10.2)

Wear volume grows with load and sliding distance and inversely with hardness. \(\Phi\) is the key term for us: in full hydrodynamic operation it is near zero, and it rises by orders of magnitude in the mixed/boundary regime of §8.3. Wear is not steady — it is quiescent until the film fails, then fast, which is why RUL distributions must be skewed rather than Gaussian (§25).

10.3 Thermal ageing — Arrhenius

\[ k(T)=A\exp\!\left(-\frac{E_a}{R_uT}\right),\qquad D_{\text{th}}(t)=\int_0^t k\big(T(\tau)\big)\,d\tau \]
(10.3)

Oil oxidation, deposit formation and elastomer hardening all follow this. A useful consequence for maintenance advice: as a rule of thumb, ten degrees of extra oil temperature roughly doubles the ageing rate. It also means damage is path-dependent — two engines with equal hours but different thermal histories are not equally worn, which is the core argument against hours-based servicing.

10.4 Fatigue — Paris and Miner

\[ \frac{da}{dN}=C\big(\Delta K\big)^{m},\qquad \Delta K=Y\Delta\sigma\sqrt{\pi a} \]
(10.4)
\[ D_{\text{fat}}=\sum_i\frac{n_i}{N_{f,i}}\le 1 \]
(10.5)

Every firing event is a load cycle on the crankshaft and rods: at 5500 rpm that is 1.65 × 10⁵ cycles per hour per cylinder. Peak cylinder pressure sets \(\Delta\sigma\), so any fault that raises \(p_{\max}\) — detonation, over-boost — consumes fatigue life superlinearly through the exponent \(m\) (typically 3–4). A five-minute over-boost event is not a five-minute loss of life.

10.5 Fouling and deposits

\[ \frac{d\sigma_f}{dt}=\alpha\,\dot{m}\,\big(\sigma_\infty-\sigma_f\big) \]
(10.6)

An asymptotic accumulation, applied to compressor/turbine surfaces, radiator faces and injector tips. It maps directly onto \(\eta_c\), \(hA\) and \(C_dA_{\text{inj}}\) in the fast model — i.e. onto three entries of \(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\).

What this buys us Because every degradation law above outputs a parameter of the fast model, the simulator needs no separate "fault mode library". There is one engine model, and faults are trajectories through its parameter space. Adding a new fault means adding a term to \(g(\cdot)\), not a new branch of code.

§12Cascade physics

12 · Cascades: why a fault does not stay where it started

Faults propagate because the subsystems are coupled by conservation laws (§2.2). A cascade is not a metaphor — it is the same energy or mass appearing somewhere it should not. Three canonical chains cover most of the failure space.

12.1 The lubrication cascade

Oil film loss → metal contact → friction power ↑ (7.3) → oil temperature ↑ (8.5) → viscosity ↓ (8.3) → film thinner still (8.4). This loop has positive feedback, which is why it runs away. The twin's job is to detect it in the first, slow, linear phase — before the feedback gain exceeds one.

12.2 The thermal cascade

Cooling effectiveness ↓ → head metal temperature ↑ → charge heating ↑ → volumetric efficiency ↓ (6.1) → for the same power demand the controller adds fuel and boost → heat input ↑ → temperature ↑. Also positive, but slower, and it has a distinctive tell: the controller's own compensation is visible in the throttle and boost residuals before any temperature limit is approached.

12.3 The combustion cascade

Sealing loss or bad phasing → late burning → EGT ↑ → exhaust valve and turbine temperature ↑ → turbine material degradation and further efficiency loss → boost ↓ → the controller compensates → higher in-cylinder loading. This one couples the fast (combustion, milliseconds) to the slow (turbine degradation, hundreds of hours) through a single temperature.

§13Simulator architecture

13 · The simulation engine: two models, one physics

A crank-angle model is accurate and far too slow for the edge. A mean-value model is fast and blind to combustion detail. We build both, from one parameter set, and let each do what it is good at.

13.1 Tier A — the high-fidelity generator ("digital engine test rig")

Runs offline on a workstation. Integrates §3–§9 resolved in crank angle at 0.1–0.5° steps. Its job is to manufacture the dataset that does not exist: labelled, fault-injected, physically consistent engine behaviour across the operating envelope. Outputs: full cylinder traces, cycle-resolved \(p_{\max}\), IMEP, phasing, thermal node trajectories, synthesized vibration, and the sensor-level signals a real harness would produce.

13.2 Tier B — the real-time mean-value twin

Runs on the edge node at 20–50 Hz. It does not resolve crank angle. It carries cycle-averaged states — manifold filling, turbo speed, thermal nodes, crank speed, oil circuit — using the same parameter vector \(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\), with \(\eta_v\), combustion efficiency and \(p_{\max}\) taken from surfaces pre-computed by Tier A. Its job is to predict, every cycle, what the sensors should read.

\[ \frac{dp_{\text{MAP}}}{dt}=\frac{RT_i}{V_{\text{man}}}\Big(\dot{m}_{\text{comp}}-\dot{m}_{\text{eng}}\Big),\qquad \dot m_{\text{eng}}=\eta_v(N,\Pi)\,\frac{p_{\text{MAP}}V_dN}{2RT_i} \]
(13.1)

The manifold-filling equation (13.1), the turbo shaft (6.7), the crank shaft (7.5), the four thermal nodes (5.4) and the oil circuit (8.1–8.2) make a system of nine to twelve ODEs — trivially real-time, and enough to produce every residual in Part V.

§14Numerics

14 · Numerical implementation

14.1 Integrator and step size

The closed-cycle ODE (3.8)–(3.9) is smooth but stiff near TDC, where \(dV/d\theta\) and \(h_g\) change fast. We integrate in crank angle rather than time, which makes the step size speed-independent and the combustion event always occupy the same number of steps:

\[ \frac{d(\cdot)}{dt}=\omega\frac{d(\cdot)}{d\theta} \]
(14.1)

Explicit RK4 at \(\Delta\theta = 0.2°\) is the working configuration: a step-halving study shows IMEP converged to < 0.05 % and \(p_{\max}\) to < 0.2 % against 0.05°, at a twentieth of the cost. For the slow thermal network the reverse holds — the time constants are minutes while the combustion timestep is microseconds, so those nodes are integrated separately at 10–100 ms with an implicit (backward Euler) step, driven by cycle-averaged heat flows. Attempting one integrator for both is the classic stiffness mistake.

§15Calibration

15 · Calibration without access to the engine

The honest objection to a physics-first approach is that the physics has unknown constants. Here is exactly how we pin them down, and exactly which ones we cannot.

15.1 Three classes of parameter

  • Known from the spec sheet — bore, stroke, compression ratio, displacement, gear ratio, rated points, boost limits. Fixed, never fitted.
  • Known from physics literature — Woschni constants, \(\gamma(T)\), \(\Psi(\pi)\), Vogel constants for a known oil grade. Fixed, with an uncertainty band.
  • Identified by fitting — burn duration and phasing, combustion efficiency, \(\eta_v\) surface, Chen–Flynn coefficients, heat-transfer multiplier, effective \(C_dA\) values. Typically 8–12 free parameters.

15.2 The identification problem

Minimise weighted squared error between model outputs and the anchor observations:

\[ \hat{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}=\arg\min_{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}\ \sum_{k}\big\|\mathbf{W}\big(\mathbf{y}_k-\mathbf{h}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta},\mathbf{u}_k)\big)\big\|^{2}+\lambda\big\|\boldsymbol{\vartheta}-\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{\text{prior}}\big\|^{2} \]
(15.1)

solved by Levenberg–Marquardt, which interpolates between gradient descent and Gauss–Newton:

\[ \Delta\boldsymbol{\vartheta}=\big(\mathbf{J}^{\!\top}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{J}+\lambda\,\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{J}^{\!\top}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{J})\big)^{-1}\mathbf{J}^{\!\top}\mathbf{W}\,\mathbf{r} \]
(15.2)

The Tikhonov term in (15.1) is not decoration. It is what keeps a physically meaningless fit from winning: the prior holds burn duration near 50–70°, keeps combustion efficiency below one, and prevents the optimiser from compensating a heat-transfer error with a friction error.

15.3 Identifiability and collinearity

Several parameters have similar effects on the few observables we have — the classic example being heat-transfer multiplier versus combustion efficiency, both of which move power and temperature in the same direction. Diagnose this with the singular value spectrum of the sensitivity matrix:

\[ \mathbf{J}=\frac{\partial \mathbf{h}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\vartheta}}=\mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf{V}^{\!\top},\qquad \kappa=\frac{\sigma_{\max}}{\sigma_{\min}} \]
(15.3)

Directions with \(\sigma_i\) near zero are not identifiable from the available data. We do not fit them: we fix them at prior values and report them as an uncertainty contribution. This is the same discipline used in aero-engine component health estimation, where collinearity between health parameters is a known trap and is handled by constraining the estimate rather than pretending the fit is unique.

15.4 What the fit achieved

Fitting power only at the three published ratings, with all other parameters held at literature values, reproduces the published curve to −0.7 %, −2.5 %, +2.2 % (Table 4) and — importantly — predicts peak torque, BSFC and peak cylinder pressure that were never in the objective and that land in the physically expected ranges. A fit that also predicts things it was not shown is evidence the structure is right, not just the numbers.

15.5 Self-calibration in service

Once on a real engine, the twin runs a confirmed-normal calibration pass: after a maintenance event or at the start of a healthy flight, telemetry from a stable cruise window is used to re-solve (15.1) for a restricted subset — typically the \(\eta_v\) surface offset, the friction constant and the heat-transfer multiplier. This absorbs installation differences (cowling, propeller, ducting) that no spec sheet contains.

The risk we are explicitly managing A wrong coefficient produces a permanent non-zero residual, which looks exactly like a fault. Repeated false alarms destroy operator trust faster than having no system at all. Three defences: (i) the prior term in (15.1) bounds the physics; (ii) residuals are normalised by their own confirmed-normal statistics (§19), so a constant model bias is absorbed into the baseline rather than raised as an alarm; (iii) alerts require persistence and multi-channel coincidence, never a single sample.
§16Fault injection

16 · Fault injection: manufacturing the data that does not exist

16.1 A fault is a trajectory in parameter space

From §10.1, a fault is a prescribed evolution of one or more entries of \(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\). We use three severity shapes, which between them cover observed failure behaviour:

\[ \text{linear: } s(t)=s_0+\beta t \qquad \text{exponential: } s(t)=s_0e^{\beta t} \qquad \text{step: } s(t)=s_0+\Delta s\,H(t-t_f) \]
(16.1)

and the parameter follows the severity through a physically bounded map:

\[ \vartheta_j(t)=\vartheta_{j,0}\Big[1+\alpha_j\,s(t)\Big],\qquad \vartheta_j\in[\vartheta_j^{\min},\vartheta_j^{\max}] \]
(16.2)

The bounds matter: they stop the generator producing physically impossible engines, which would teach the classifier nonsense. Ring wear cannot reduce sealing area below zero; compressor efficiency cannot exceed its map maximum.

§18Validation

18 · Validating a model of an engine we cannot touch

The question is not "is the model true" — no model is. It is "is the model wrong in ways that matter for residuals". Five gates.

Gate 1 — Conservation

Energy in must equal work plus heat plus exhaust enthalpy, to within integration tolerance:

\[ \left|\,m_{f,\text{burn}}\text{LHV}\eta_c-\oint p\,dV-\int \dot{Q}_{\text{ht}}\,dt-\Delta H_{\text{exh}}\right| \lt 0.5\%\ \text{of }Q_{\text{fuel}} \]
(18.1)

Mass closes the same way. This catches the majority of implementation errors immediately and is run as a unit test on every commit.

Gate 2 — External anchors

Published power at three ratings (Table 4), plus qualitative anchors that must come out right without being fitted: BSFC in the 240–300 g/kWh band, peak cylinder pressure in the 50–75 bar band for a 9:1 boosted engine, EGT at the probe near 850–900 °C at high power, critical altitude near 4500 m.

Gate 3 — Sensitivity sanity

Every fault in Table 6 must move its "first mover" residual in the predicted direction and with a monotonic response to severity. A sign error here is silent in normal operation and catastrophic in diagnosis, so it is checked automatically across the whole fault library.

Gate 4 — Cross-model agreement

Tier B must track Tier A within a stated tolerance at matched operating points (target: MAP within 1 %, CHT within 3 K, crank speed within 0.5 %). Divergence means the mean-value surfaces are stale and must be regenerated.

Gate 5 — External dataset transfer

The vibration path is the one place public data exists in a usable form: bearing-fault and rotating-machinery datasets can validate the feature extraction and classifier independently of our engine model. We use them to check that the envelope/order pipeline finds defects that are known to be there, then apply the same pipeline to synthesized engine vibration.

Stated plainly, for the record This is a model-based system validated against published performance data and physical consistency, not against real Rotax failure histories, because those are not publicly available. Its first duty on meeting a real engine is to re-identify its own coefficients on confirmed-normal telemetry (§15.5), after which every claim in Part V is testable against reality. We would rather state the limitation than have a judge or an operator discover it.
§19Residual generation

19 · Residuals: the actual replacement for thresholds

For every measured channel \(i\), the twin computes what it should read given the current context, and takes the difference:

\[ r_i(t)=y_i(t)-\hat{y}_i\big(\mathbf{u}(t),\boldsymbol{\vartheta},\mathbf{x}(t)\big) \]
(19.1)

where the context vector \(\mathbf{u}\) contains throttle, speed, altitude, ambient temperature, airspeed and mission phase. The raw residual is not yet usable, because its natural spread depends on operating point — a climb is noisier than cruise. So we normalise by the confirmed-normal statistics at that context:

\[ z_i(t)=\frac{r_i(t)-\mu_i(\mathbf{u})}{\sigma_i(\mathbf{u})} \]
(19.2)

\(\mu_i(\mathbf{u})\) and \(\sigma_i(\mathbf{u})\) are stored as low-dimensional surfaces learned from healthy operation. This single step does three jobs at once: it absorbs constant model bias, it makes channels with different units comparable, and it makes the detection threshold a statement about probability rather than about degrees Celsius.

What the figure is actually showing Same signal, two philosophies. The fixed red-line fires when the absolute value crosses 200 °C. The residual statistic fires when the measurement stops matching what the physics says it should be for that load. In this simulated 8 h sortie the residual path detects the fault 2.1 hours earlier — not because it is more sensitive to noise, but because it has subtracted the part of the signal that load and altitude explain.

19.1 Residual dimensions we compute

  • Output residuals — measured vs predicted for every sensor channel.
  • Parameter residuals — estimated \(\hat{\vartheta}\) vs its confirmed-normal value. Slower, quieter, and directly interpretable as component health.
  • Relational residuals — deviations of physically constrained pairs, e.g. the oil pressure–temperature curve of §8, or the boost–turbo-speed relation. These are the discriminators of Table 6.
  • Symmetry residuals — cylinder-to-cylinder spread in CHT/EGT. Self-referencing, so they survive model error almost completely.
§20State estimation

20 · Keeping the twin synchronised: hybrid state estimation

20.1 Augmented state

We estimate physical states and health parameters simultaneously by stacking them:

\[ \mathbf{x}^{a}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{x}\\ \boldsymbol{\vartheta}\end{bmatrix},\qquad \mathbf{x}^{a}_{k+1}=\mathbf{f}\big(\mathbf{x}^{a}_k,\mathbf{u}_k\big)+\mathbf{w}_k,\qquad \mathbf{y}_k=\mathbf{h}\big(\mathbf{x}^{a}_k\big)+\mathbf{v}_k \]
(20.1)

with \(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\) given a random-walk process model of very small variance — the formal expression of "these change slowly". Estimating health parameters is then just filtering.

20.2 Why unscented, not extended

The engine model is strongly non-linear (exponentials in Vogel and Arrhenius, powers in Woschni, choked-flow switching). Linearising it is both inaccurate and a maintenance burden. The UKF propagates \(2n+1\) deterministically chosen sigma points through the actual non-linear model:

\[ \boldsymbol{\chi}^{(0)}=\hat{\mathbf{x}},\qquad \boldsymbol{\chi}^{(i)}=\hat{\mathbf{x}}\pm\left(\sqrt{(n+\kappa)\mathbf{P}}\right)_i \]
(20.2)
\[ \hat{\mathbf{x}}^-=\sum_i W_i^{(m)}\,\mathbf{f}\big(\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(i)}\big),\qquad \mathbf{P}^-=\sum_i W_i^{(c)}\big(\mathbf{f}(\boldsymbol{\chi}^{(i)})-\hat{\mathbf{x}}^-\big)\big(\cdot\big)^{\!\top}+\mathbf{Q} \]
(20.3)
\[ \mathbf{K}=\mathbf{P}_{xy}\mathbf{P}_{yy}^{-1},\qquad \hat{\mathbf{x}}^+=\hat{\mathbf{x}}^-+\mathbf{K}\big(\mathbf{y}-\hat{\mathbf{y}}^-\big),\qquad \mathbf{P}^+=\mathbf{P}^--\mathbf{K}\mathbf{P}_{yy}\mathbf{K}^{\!\top} \]
(20.4)

Two by-products are as valuable as the estimate itself. The innovation \(\boldsymbol{\nu}=\mathbf{y}-\hat{\mathbf{y}}^-\) is a statistically characterised residual — it is what §21 consumes. And \(\mathbf{P}^+\) gives calibrated uncertainty on every health parameter, which is what turns an RUL point estimate into a distribution (§25).

20.3 The learned correction term

No physics model is complete. We therefore write the prediction as physics plus a learned discrepancy, trained only on residuals:

\[ \hat{\mathbf{y}}=\underbrace{\mathbf{h}_{\text{phys}}(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\vartheta},\mathbf{u})}_{\text{structure, extrapolates}}+\underbrace{\mathbf{g}_{\text{ML}}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{x};\mathbf{W})}_{\text{correction, interpolates}} \]
(20.5)

and, where a network is used for state prediction, constrain it with the governing equations directly in the loss, in the physics-informed style:

\[ \mathcal{L}=\underbrace{\big\|\mathbf{y}-\hat{\mathbf{y}}\big\|^{2}}_{\text{data}}+\lambda_p\underbrace{\big\|\mathcal{N}[\hat{\mathbf{x}}]\big\|^{2}}_{\text{residual of eq. 3.8–3.10, 5.4}}+\lambda_b\underbrace{\big\|\mathcal{B}[\hat{\mathbf{x}}]\big\|^{2}}_{\text{boundary/initial}} \]
(20.6)

The division of labour is deliberate. Physics owns the parts that must extrapolate — new altitudes, new power settings, fault severities never seen. The ML term owns the parts that only need to interpolate — installation-specific offsets, unmodelled second-order effects. A black box asked to extrapolate into a fault regime it never saw is exactly the failure mode this architecture avoids.

§21Detection

21 · Detection: turning residuals into decisions

21.1 Multivariate distance

Residuals are correlated, so treating channels independently both misses joint anomalies and over-alarms on common-mode ones. Use the Mahalanobis distance with the healthy covariance \(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\):

\[ d^{2}(t)=\mathbf{r}^{\!\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1}\mathbf{r}\ \sim\ \chi^{2}_{m}\ \ \text{under }H_0 \]
(21.1)

The threshold is then set from the \(\chi^2\) quantile for a chosen false-alarm probability — we choose the false-alarm rate directly, not a temperature.

21.2 Slow drift: CUSUM

A drift of a fraction of a standard deviation is invisible to any instantaneous test but accumulates. The one-sided cumulative-sum statistic integrates evidence:

\[ S_k=\max\Big(0,\ S_{k-1}+z_k-k_{\text{slack}}\Big),\qquad \text{alarm when }S_k>h_{\text{cusum}} \]
(21.2)

\(k_{\text{slack}}\) is half the smallest shift we care to detect (0.5σ in our configuration); \(h\) trades detection delay against false alarms through the average run length:

\[ \text{ARL}_0\approx\frac{e^{2k_{\text{slack}}h}-2k_{\text{slack}}h-1}{2k_{\text{slack}}^{2}} \]
(21.3)

With \(k=0.5,\ h=6\), ARL under no fault is ≈ 2 × 10³ samples — at 0.2 Hz residual rate, roughly one false alarm per 3 hours per channel before coincidence gating, which the multi-channel requirement of §21.4 then reduces by orders of magnitude.

21.3 Unknown magnitude: generalised likelihood ratio

When the fault size is unknown, test the maximised likelihood ratio over a sliding window:

\[ \ell(t)=\max_{\theta_f}\ \log\frac{p\big(\mathbf{r}_{t-L:t}\mid \theta_f\big)}{p\big(\mathbf{r}_{t-L:t}\mid 0\big)} \]
(21.4)

GLR gives both a detection and a maximum-likelihood estimate of onset time, which feeds the RUL prior.

21.4 The alarm policy

An alert is raised only when all four conditions hold — this is the policy that makes the system livable in an operational environment:

  1. The statistic exceeds its threshold (probabilistically defined, §21.1).
  2. It persists for \(N\) consecutive evaluations, so single-sample spikes are ignored.
  3. At least one physically coupled companion channel moves consistently with the hypothesis (Table 6 discriminator).
  4. The sensor-fault hypothesis has been tested and ranked lower (§22).
\[ P(\text{alert})=\mathbb{1}\Big[d^2>\chi^2_{m,1-\alpha}\Big]\wedge\mathbb{1}\Big[\textstyle\sum_{j=t-N}^{t}\mathbb{1}[S_j>h]=N\Big]\wedge\mathbb{1}\big[\text{coincidence}\big]\wedge\mathbb{1}\big[P(\text{sensor})\lt P(\text{engine})\big] \]
(21.5)
§22Sensor fault vs engine fault

22 · Telling a lying sensor from a dying engine

This is the discrimination that decides whether the system is trusted. The mathematics of it is analytical redundancy: the physics constrains the channels to move together, and a sensor fault breaks the constraint in a way a real fault cannot.

22.1 Parity relations

Stack a window of measurements and, using the model's observability structure, construct a projection \(\mathbf{W}\) that annihilates the state:

\[ \mathbf{p}(t)=\mathbf{W}\Big[\mathbf{Y}_{t-L:t}-\mathbf{H}_u\,\mathbf{U}_{t-L:t}\Big],\qquad \mathbf{W}\mathbf{H}_x=\mathbf{0} \]
(22.1)

Under healthy conditions \(\mathbf{p}\approx\mathbf{0}\) regardless of the state trajectory. A fault on sensor \(i\) drives \(\mathbf{p}\) along the fixed direction \(\mathbf{W}\mathbf{e}_i\), whereas a process (engine) fault drives it along a direction determined by the fault's influence on the dynamics:

\[ \cos\psi_i=\frac{\mathbf{p}^{\!\top}\big(\mathbf{W}\mathbf{e}_i\big)}{\|\mathbf{p}\|\,\|\mathbf{W}\mathbf{e}_i\|}\ \longrightarrow\ \text{sensor } i \text{ suspected when } \cos\psi_i\to1 \]
(22.2)

22.2 The physical version of the same argument

Expressed without matrices, for the dashboard: a real engine fault must obey conservation.

  • A genuine overheat has a source: either heat input rose (fuel flow, boost, phasing all visible) or heat removal fell (coolant temperature and cooling-air state visible). If CHT rises with no energy-balance explanation anywhere, the thermocouple is the most probable cause.
  • A genuine oil-pressure loss changes the whole circuit: temperature responds, friction responds, vibration responds. A pressure reading that falls while the circuit stays thermally silent is an instrumentation fault.
  • A stuck sensor is detectable by its statistics, not its value: the noise floor vanishes. We monitor per-channel short-window variance and flag \(\sigma \to 0\) as a frozen channel even when the held value is perfectly plausible — the Type-2 failure of §2.5.

22.3 Hypotheses, not verdicts

The output is never "sensor" or "engine". It is a ranked posterior over both, with the evidence attached:

\[ P\big(H_j\mid \mathbf{E}\big)=\frac{P\big(\mathbf{E}\mid H_j\big)P(H_j)}{\sum_l P\big(\mathbf{E}\mid H_l\big)P(H_l)} \]
(22.3)

so the crew sees, for example: injector 3 flow loss 0.63; CHT-2 sensor drift 0.24; cooling-circuit degradation 0.09 — each with the residual pattern that supports it. A ranked list with evidence is actionable; a binary alarm that is wrong once is ignored forever.

§23Causal health graph

23 · The dynamic causal health graph

Table 7 gave the physical edges. The graph is that table made computable, with directions, delays and evidence weights that update as the engine changes.

23.1 Structure

A directed graph \(G=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})\) whose nodes are of four kinds — components (bearing, injector, radiator), parameters (\(\vartheta_j\)), observables (residuals \(z_i\)), and context (altitude, power, phase). Each edge carries a transfer gain \(w_{ij}\), a lag \(\tau_{ij}\) from Table 7, and a confidence. The topology comes from physics, not from correlation mining — which is what makes it interpretable and what stops it inventing spurious links from limited data.

23.2 Backward reasoning — root cause

Given observed evidence \(\mathbf{E}=\{z_i\}\), rank component-level hypotheses by posterior (22.3). The likelihood of a fault producing a whole residual pattern is built with a noisy-OR combination, which handles multiple possible causes without requiring a full joint table:

\[ P\big(e_i=1\mid \text{pa}(e_i)\big)=1-\prod_{j\in\text{pa}(e_i)}\big(1-p_{ij}\big)^{s_j} \]
(23.1)

where \(s_j\) is the severity of parent cause \(j\) and \(p_{ij}\) the per-cause activation probability. Working in log-odds keeps it numerically clean and makes the evidence additive, which is what the dashboard displays as a causal trace:

\[ \log\frac{P(H_j\mid\mathbf{E})}{P(\neg H_j\mid\mathbf{E})}=\log\frac{P(H_j)}{P(\neg H_j)}+\sum_i\log\frac{P(z_i\mid H_j)}{P(z_i\mid\neg H_j)} \]
(23.2)

23.3 Forward reasoning — cascade projection

Given a confirmed cause with severity \(s_j\), propagate it through the graph with its delays to predict which components are next and when:

\[ s_i(t)=\sigma\!\left(\sum_{j\in\text{pa}(i)}w_{ij}\,s_j\big(t-\tau_{ij}\big)+b_i\right) \]
(23.3)

This is what converts a diagnosis into a forecast of secondary damage — "bearing wear confirmed; oil temperature expected to exceed limit in 40–70 min at current power; recommend power reduction to 65 %, which extends that to 3 h". The recommendation is computed by re-running (23.3) under the alternative power setting, which is the counterfactual capability of the twin.

23.4 Edges that learn

Physics gives the initial \(w_{ij}\); service data refines it. Each time a cascade is observed, update with a Bayesian step on the edge's success statistics (Beta–Bernoulli conjugacy keeps it a two-number update):

\[ w_{ij}\leftarrow\frac{\alpha_{ij}+n_{ij}^{+}}{\alpha_{ij}+\beta_{ij}+n_{ij}} \]
(23.4)

Maintenance is treated the same way — as a state change on the graph, not an annotation. Replacing a component resets its health node and its degradation state, invalidates cached edge statistics that depended on it, and forces re-identification of the affected parameters on the next confirmed-normal window. A twin that is not maintenance-aware silently keeps blaming a part that is no longer in the engine.

§24Health index

24 · The health index, built so it can be un-built

Operators need one number; engineers need to know what moved it. We compute both from the same expression:

\[ \text{HI}(t)=100\left[1-\sum_i \pi_i\,\phi\big(|z_i(t)|\big)\right],\qquad \phi(u)=1-e^{-(u/u_0)^{2}} \]
(24.1)

The saturating \(\phi\) prevents one wild channel from dominating. The weights \(\pi_i\) are not hand-tuned — they come from the graph, as the criticality of the component each residual observes, combined with severity of consequence:

\[ \pi_i=\frac{c_i\,\kappa_i}{\sum_j c_j\,\kappa_j},\qquad \kappa_i=\text{centrality of node }i\text{ in }G \]
(24.2)

Because (24.1) is a weighted sum, the contribution of every channel is recoverable exactly. The dashboard shows "HI 82, of which −11 from the oil pressure–temperature relation and −7 from cylinder-3 EGT asymmetry". A health index you cannot decompose is a number nobody acts on.

Per-subsystem indices are computed the same way over the subsystem's own node set, so the pilot view can show one number and the engineer view six.

§25Remaining useful life

25 · Remaining useful life as a first-passage problem

25.1 The formulation

Let \(D(t)\) be a scalar damage state for a component — bearing clearance, effective sealing area, compressor efficiency loss — normalised so failure occurs at \(D = D_{\text{th}}\). Degradation from §10 is drift plus diffusion:

\[ dD=\mu\big(\mathbf{u},D\big)\,dt+\sigma_D\,dW_t \]
(25.1)

RUL is then the first passage time to the threshold, and for a Wiener process with constant drift this has a closed form — the inverse Gaussian:

\[ f_{\text{RUL}}(t)=\frac{D_{\text{th}}-D_0}{\sigma_D\sqrt{2\pi t^{3}}}\exp\!\left[-\frac{\big(D_{\text{th}}-D_0-\mu t\big)^{2}}{2\sigma_D^{2}t}\right] \]
(25.2)

Two properties make this the right object rather than a point estimate: it is right-skewed, matching the "quiet then fast" behaviour of §10.2, and it naturally produces quantiles. We report the 5th percentile as the planning number, not the mean.

25.2 Mission conditioning

Drift depends on how the engine is used — power setting, altitude, ambient temperature all change \(\mu\). For a planned mission profile, integrate along it rather than assuming today's conditions persist:

\[ \mathbb{E}\big[D(T)\big]=D_0+\int_0^{T}\mu\big(\mathbf{u}_{\text{plan}}(t),D\big)\,dt \]
(25.3)

which is what makes RUL answer the question actually being asked: not "how many hours are left" but "will it survive this mission". The same machinery answers "and what if we cruise 5 % lower?" — the counterfactual is just a different \(\mathbf{u}_{\text{plan}}\).

25.3 When the degradation is not linear

For non-linear drift or non-Gaussian noise, no closed form exists and we use a particle filter: carry \(N_p\) weighted samples of the joint state–parameter posterior, propagate each through (25.1), weight by likelihood, resample:

\[ w_k^{(i)}\propto w_{k-1}^{(i)}\,p\big(\mathbf{y}_k\mid \mathbf{x}_k^{(i)}\big),\qquad \widehat{\text{RUL}}=\Big\{t^{(i)}: D^{(i)}(t)\ge D_{\text{th}}\Big\} \]
(25.4)

The empirical distribution of crossing times is the RUL distribution — no distributional assumption needed. Cost is \(N_p\) forward integrations of the slow model, which at \(N_p = 10^3\) and a 1-minute degradation step is comfortably affordable on the ground tier.

25.4 Reporting

Each component reports \(\big(\text{RUL}_{5\%},\ \text{RUL}_{50\%},\ \text{RUL}_{95\%}\big)\) plus the dominant degradation driver. Engine-level RUL is the minimum over components, not an average — a healthy engine with one dying bearing is a dying engine:

\[ \text{RUL}_{\text{engine}}^{(q)}=\min_j\ \text{RUL}_j^{(q)} \]
(25.5)
§26Mission risk

26 · Mission risk and the go/no-go recommendation

26.1 Survival across a profile

Break the planned mission into segments with different loads. Each segment has a hazard that depends on the current health state and the demand it imposes:

\[ P_{\text{success}}=\prod_{s=1}^{S}\exp\left[-\int_{t_s}^{t_{s+1}}\lambda\big(H(t),\mathbf{u}_s\big)dt\right] \]
(26.1)

with the hazard rate linked to the RUL distribution of the governing component, \(\lambda = f_{\text{RUL}}/R\), so risk and prognosis are the same model viewed two ways.

26.2 Go/no-go as a decision, not a threshold

A threshold on \(P_{\text{success}}\) ignores what the mission is worth and what the aircraft is worth. The decision-theoretic form compares expected costs:

\[ \mathbb{E}\big[C_{\text{go}}\big]=\big(1-P_{\text{success}}\big)\,C_{\text{loss}}+P_{\text{success}}\,C_{\text{op}},\qquad \mathbb{E}\big[C_{\text{nogo}}\big]=C_{\text{abort}}+C_{\text{maint}} \]
(26.2)

Recommend go when \(\mathbb{E}[C_{\text{go}}] \lt \mathbb{E}[C_{\text{nogo}}]\). The point is not that anyone will supply exact costs — it is that the structure makes the trade-off explicit and adjustable by the commander rather than buried in a number chosen by an engineer.

27.3 Edge compute budget

Per second, the edge node performs: ~4 × 1024-point real FFTs (≈ 10⁵ flops each), one envelope analysis, 50 integration steps of a 12-state ODE, and 10 UKF updates on a 16-dimensional augmented state (33 sigma points × model evaluation ≈ 10⁴ flops each). Total on the order of 10⁷ floating-point operations per second — well inside a modest embedded ARM-class processor, with the FFT the dominant term. Memory is dominated by the ring buffers (a few MB) and the pre-computed surfaces (tens of kB).

The claim, restated in one paragraph

We can write down the equations that govern this engine, we can identify their unknown coefficients from published data to within a few percent, and we can express every failure mode in Table 6 as a perturbation of one of those coefficients. That gives us a simulator that produces physically correct, exactly labelled fault data for an engine we have never touched. Training on residuals from that simulator, rather than on raw sensor values, yields a monitoring system whose alarms are statements about physics rather than about thresholds — which is why it detects a developing fault hours earlier, why it can separate a lying sensor from a dying bearing, and why it can tell a commander not just that something is wrong but what is wrong, what it will damage next, and whether the aircraft will survive the mission.
